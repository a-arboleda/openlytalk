# OpenlyTalk Intentional Communication Framework

> **Status**: Confirmed story-first product direction; implementation pending  
> **Version**: 1.2  
> **Confirmed**: 2026-09-02

## Purpose

OpenlyTalk's promise is **Communicate more intentionally in English**. The beta
lets learners speak freely or respond to one approved story question, then
offers concise feedback that helps them notice one observable choice in how
they expressed their meaning.

The question opens a door to a real story. It does not contain the lesson,
explain the desired insight, or tell the learner what the answer reveals about
their identity.

## Learner outcome

A useful practice helps the learner:

1. Speak about a real moment, memory, person, decision, change, or experience.
2. Notice one observable choice in how they communicated it.
3. Try one meaning-preserving adjustment in real life.

The coach may reflect a reason, value, feeling, or identity statement only when
the learner expressed it. It never infers a fixed personality, hidden motive,
mental state, diagnosis, or true identity.

## Two ways to begin

The learner chooses between:

- **Free sharing** — speak about anything they want to share.
- **Random question** — receive one story question selected from a flat,
  team-approved catalog.

There are no learner-visible categories or families. The learner does not
select a communication skill, technique, rubric, or lesson.

Free sharing is not an open chatbot. It still consists of one spoken response
and one feedback result, with no AI reply or continuing conversation.

## Story-question principle

A story question points toward one concrete experience and includes a natural
time anchor. Useful anchors include **recently**, **the last time**, **in the
past month**, **in the past six months**, **in the past year**, **when you were
younger**, **from childhood**, and **a few years ago**.

The anchor makes recall easier and reduces abstract answers. It should not make
the experience so narrow that many learners have nothing to say.

Questions use familiar B1-B2 language, normally 6–14 words and never more than
16. They may ask about a moment, memory, person, change, decision, first
experience, or recent event. They never state what the story is supposed to
prove about the learner.

AI does not generate or alter learner-visible questions. Selection comes from
the approved flat catalog and may exclude recently used question IDs.

## Practice journeys

### Free sharing

1. Choose free sharing.
2. See a short static invitation to share anything.
3. Record one English response.
4. Review local audio and the transcript; re-record if needed.
5. Submit once and receive concision-only text feedback.
6. Reopen the submitted transcript from feedback.

### Random question

1. Choose a random question.
2. Receive one approved story question.
3. Optionally play it once, open its reviewed guide, or replace it.
4. Record one English response.
5. Review local audio and the transcript; re-record if needed.
6. Submit once and receive grounded text feedback.
7. Reopen the question and transcript from feedback.

## Coach mirror

Free sharing returns only **Keep it concise**: one transcript-grounded
observation, one next step when trimming would help, and optionally one shorter
meaning-preserving example. It does not classify the topic or add a coaching
lens, general improvement, English polish, or real-life action.

Random-question feedback remains concise and communication-first:

1. **What you practiced**
2. **What worked**
3. **One improvement**
4. **Keep it concise**
5. **A natural example**
6. **English polish** — zero to two useful alternatives
7. **Try it in real life**

Private reviewed metadata can guide random-question feedback. It never guides
free sharing because that mode has no question record or general coaching
focus.

Feedback can notice clarity, organization, relevant detail, sequence, humor,
directness, expressed reactions, or how the learner framed a person or event.
It describes what happened **in this response**. It does not label the learner
as funny, reserved, dramatic, direct, reflective, confident, or another stable
type of person.

Every response receives one evidence-based concision observation. The coach
names either what kept the story focused or one specific repetition, detour, or
detail that could be reduced. It does not confuse brevity with quality or ask
the learner to remove context needed to understand the story.

## Editorial and safety boundaries

- The learner decides what to share and how personal to be.
- Childhood questions remain neutral and never assume childhood was happy.
- Questions about work, study, travel, family, or money must not assume the
  learner has that experience.
- A time window should support recall without manufacturing an experience.
- Potentially sensitive questions remain easy to replace.
- Questions, private coaching metadata, rubrics, and provider instructions
  remain server-owned.

## Acceptance criteria before implementation

- The flat catalog contains enough fully approved questions for useful variety.
- Every exact wording has cofounder approval, a stable ID, and a version.
- Every question has one story invitation, a natural time anchor, familiar
  B1-B2 language, and no more than 16 words.
- Every selectable question has complete compatible private coaching metadata.
- Selection excludes the current and bounded recent question IDs.
- Free sharing never invents a question or pre-classifies the learner's topic.
- Feedback fixtures prove evidence grounding and prohibit identity inference.
- Both one-response journeys pass mobile, accessibility, privacy, and failure
  verification.
