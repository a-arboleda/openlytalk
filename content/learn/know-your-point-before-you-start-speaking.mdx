Most unclear answers do not begin with a grammar problem. They begin before the
first sentence: the speaker has not decided what the listener should understand.

## Start with the meaning

Before you speak, try to finish this private sentence:

> The main thing I want this person to understand is…

You do not need to say that sentence aloud. It simply gives your response a
direction. Once you know the point, you can choose which details help it.

## Notice the difference

Imagine someone asks why you changed your weekend plans.

### A response that searches for the point

> Well, first we talked about going on Saturday, although it was not completely
> decided, and my friend had mentioned another place before, and then the
> weather forecast changed…

The details may all be true, but the listener has to wait to understand the
reason for the story.

### A response with direction

> We changed our plans because the weather looked bad. We chose an indoor place
> instead.

The shorter version works because the reason appears early. More detail can be
added if the listener needs it.

## A point is not the whole story

Knowing your point does not mean removing every detail. A useful detail can
make your meaning clearer, more human, or easier to remember. The question is
not “Can I make this shorter?” It is “Does this detail help the listener
understand what I mean?”

## Try this

Think of something small that happened today. Before describing it, write one
sentence that begins with:

> The main thing I want you to understand is…

Then explain what happened in two or three sentences. Keep the details that
support your point.

## Takeaway

Clear speaking often begins with one quiet decision: know what you want the
listener to understand.
